Title: Compile package fns with the native compiler, from its source, and report what agrees with the interpreter

Numbers first, because they went down and that is expected. #5690 reported 63% of package fns compiling (3,242 of 5,138) against an April fork of the compiler with a seam that let the interpreter serve every effect and every stdlib fn the compiler lacked. This branch runs Paul's current compiler on its own, with its own stdlib and no seam: 49% compiled before the fixes (2,509 of 5,171), 54% now (2,770 of 5,174). The gap is almost entirely his stdlib surface: 2,037 of the 2,404 that don't compile hang off a Stdlib module his stdlib doesn't have, and 240 off our own `Builtin.*`; of the fns not blocked that way, 94% compile (1,843 of 1,970), up from 80%. Agreement with the interpreter went from 1,961 identical to 2,361 of 2,423 run both ways, crashes from 20 to 1, hangs from 37 to 0.

Replaces #5690's approach. That branch vendored an April fork of the compiler into the tree with a 2,000-line patch on top and a ProgramTypes-to-AST bridge; every upstream move meant re-porting the patch. This one keeps no compiler source in the repo at all: `backend/src/LibCompiler/` is gitignored, `scripts/build/vendor-compiler ~/code/compiler [commit]` copies pbiggar/darklang-compiler's `src/DarkCompiler` in from a checkout, and only `vendor/VENDORED-FROM` (repo, commit, subject) is tracked. The default build never sees it; `-p:DarkWithCompiler=true` on Cli or LocalExec builds it as a library and defines `DARK_WITH_COMPILER`, which adds the `Builtins.Compiler` project. Fixes to the compiler go to Paul as a PR (18 of them are up now, from this sweep); nothing is patched on the way in.

What's in it: `Builtins.Compiler` is six thin builtins (parse, layout, respell, compile, compile-and-run, info). `packages/darklang/compiler/sweep.dark` is where the work is: it takes any package fn, prints it with its whole closure as source his compiler accepts (fully qualified names, his spellings for Option/Result/Dict, second names of a content-addressed type as aliases), synthesizes arguments for it, runs it in the interpreter and in the compiled binary, and diffs the JSON. `scripts/compiler/report` drives that over the tree in dependency order with a pool of persistent worker processes (about 30 minutes, 6 workers capped at 4 GB each; `--only Darklang.Cli.Packages` is a minute) and writes `docs/compiler/coverage/<date>.md`, a `.tsv` and a per-function `.csv`. `scripts/compiler/upstream-tests` runs his suite inside our container. `packages/darklang/compilerCases/` are 109 small fns pinning shapes that broke, all but one matching now.

The rest of the numbers (`docs/compiler/coverage/2026-09-18-upstream.md`): the 31 that differ are float formatting, host effects or randomness, plus one real miscompile fixed in his repo since. The missing Stdlib modules are LocalStore, Cli.Tui, FileSystem, Env, HttpClient and Pretty; the 127 non-compiling fns that are the compiler's own are listed on his PR.

Worth review: the name respelling in `Builtin.compilerRespell` (it rewrites our spellings to his, quote-aware, on whole names only; two harness artifacts got into an early run through it), the argument synthesizer's choices (depth 4, one-element nested lists, `5L`/`"hello"`), and the worker protocol in `_report.py` (stdin lines in, `##done##` out, a dead worker treated as a timeout and the batch halved). The 60s budgets and the 4 GB cap are there because the compiler used to hang on some inputs and one worker once reached 39 GB.

Most of the line count is data: the coverage CSVs (this run and yesterday's, plus the April-fork runs under `archive/` for the record) and the old patch archived under `vendor/archive/`. AGENTS.md has a section on the loop. Nothing runs in CI; a job that vendors his repo at the pinned commit and runs `report --only` on one namespace is the obvious follow-up once his PR lands.
